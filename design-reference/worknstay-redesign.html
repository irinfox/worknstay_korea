Turn 6 — your notes applied
Order, width, and a phone region page
Turn 5 only carried the new style across the homepage. The region page was still at draft width, its sections were in the wrong order, and there was no phone layout for it. All fixed here — the visual language itself is unchanged.
Order and width
Region page is 1280px now, same as the homepage. New order on both viewports: cover gallery → map → guides → cities → costs and festivals side by side → attractions → dishes. Festivals are no longer above the cities, and they keep the pairing you liked: costs left, festivals right, in one tinted band that does the colour-divider job.
Phone
Homepage: the cover photo runs behind the header, headline and calculator, with the calculator card overlapping its lower edge, and the regions are a swipeable carousel with dots. Region page (5d): no cover — dark nav strip, breadcrumb, then the headline block on white, same as desktop, with the cities as a swipeable gallery.
Clickable, and the battery is back
You were right that it wasn't readable: every region card now ends with an "Open Gangwon-do →" row, and the map says to click. Two routes into a region page, both signposted. Study cafés use the four-cell battery from the old design again — filled cells for how many there are, plus the label.
Your call
The calculator lost about 90px of height: salary, age and the button share one row, and the salary field is only as wide as the number needs. Your orange is in: a 56px rule above every page headline, outbound guide links, festival dates and the spicy tag — #E08A2E for the rules, #C2531A where it carries text. About me (6a) and the Gyeonggi decline counties (6c) are now in this style too, desktop and phone. The region map now uses line pictograms — city, mountain, surf, ski — instead of dots and diamonds. Hi, I'm Irina

{{ c }}
I've lived in Korea as a Korean language student and previously worked remotely from different countries. I was more of a traveller with a laptop than a proper digital nomad, but I kinda know the vibe.
I've never worked like this in Korea and for now I'm based in Seoul, but I've travelled through all six major regions. Most of the cities you'll see here are either places I've been to or would like to visit myself.
With Korea making its digital nomad visa more accessible, I wanted to show what Korea outside Seoul is like. The language barrier and the older population in regional areas are real — with exceptions like Busan and Jeju — but good internet and apps like Google Translate and Korea's own Papago make it doable.
So please enjoy the website, choose the region that fits your lifestyle with my vibe tags, and don't forget to try the regional dishes.

Regions / Gyeonggi-do / Decline counties

Where Gyeonggi-do still qualifies

경기도 인구감소지역
Gyeonggi-do is a capital-area region, so the F-1-D income threshold there is the country's highest. Seven counties are the exception.
They sit inside the capital area administratively, but the government lists them as population-decline or watch-list areas — so they are assessed at the lower, non-capital threshold. Everything else in Gyeonggi-do and Incheon uses the capital figure.
₩70,000,000 qualifies you in these seven counties. The same figure fails everywhere else in Gyeonggi-do, where you'd need ₩78,620,000.
